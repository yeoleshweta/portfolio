# Publishing Notes — Read Before Posting

## 1. Confidentiality — needs your judgement

The case study currently contains material pulled directly from an internal John Deere research board:

- **Real participant names** (David Dean, Brad Friest, Bryan Anfinsion, Brian Mahoney, Aaron Phelps, Keith Schrader, Jan Barendt, Alexander Leuchs, Karl Hoffmann)
- **Verbatim internal quotes** about tool weaknesses
- **Internal product and process names** (Team Contract, PDP phases, AFE reporting, ACR, FPM, PUC)

For a public portfolio, the safe default is to anonymise:

- `David Dean, Product Owner` → `Product Owner`
- `Brad Friest, PM` → `Program Manager (Participant 2)`
- Keep the quotes — they're the strongest material — but attribute by role, not name.

If you have written clearance from John Deere to use names, keep them. Otherwise anonymise before publishing. **Do not publish participant names on the assumption it's fine because the work is old.**

Consider also whether screen captures showing real program numbers, budget figures, or product codes need blurring.

## 3. Missing — outcomes

The case study has no results section, because the research board contains no post-launch measurement. This is the most common gap interviewers push on.

If you have any of the following, add an **Outcome** section before Reflection:

- Adoption change after release
- Reduction in time-to-complete a team contract
- Qualitative feedback from the same participants post-change
- Anything from a follow-up study

If you have nothing, say so honestly in the Reflection — *"the engagement ended at design handoff; I'd want to have measured X"* reads far better than silence, and shows you know what should have been measured.

## 4. Suggested additions

- **Timeline / duration** — how long the project ran
- **Team** — who else was involved, and what specifically was yours
- **Constraints** — what you couldn't change (legacy data model, Fuel DS limits, release timing)

Constraints are worth adding. They're what turn a case study from "here's what I designed" into "here's what I designed given what I had."
